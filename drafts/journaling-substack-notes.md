# 10 Substack note drafts — "I am begging you to start journaling"

Source essay: Eden note "I am begging you to start journaling."
Templates: top Substack productivity notes in Eden's index, ranked by outlier score (how far each beat its creator's usual post).

## Core principles pulled from the essay
1. Scatter isn't fixed by more input. It's fixed by laying out the pieces you already have (the jigsaw puzzle).
2. The page receives *and* takes away. Once a thought is written down, it stops weighing on you.
3. The greatest minds journaled. *Meditations* was Marcus Aurelius's private journal.
4. One sentence a day is enough. "No time" doesn't hold up next to daily scrolling.
5. There's no wrong way. If you have thoughts, you can journal.
6. It gives no dopamine hit, and that's the point: it trains presence without stimulation.
7. Prompts are a compass, not a map.
8. Proof: 10 scattered years, a Moleskine dropped after 3 days, now 4 years in, and a better husband and father.

## 1. Template: "Stop consuming, start living" (@emilyvermillion, 1,503x, 20.7k likes)
**Structure:** a hard truth, then short imperatives, a one-line beat, and a closing contrast.

> At some point you have to stop collecting new interests and actually sit with the ones you have.
>
> Close the tab. Skip the course. Put down the new hobby.
>
> Pick up a pen. Write one sentence. Name what's bothering you.
>
> Read it back.
>
> Another idea can distract you from the scatter, but only the page can show you the pattern.

## 2. Template: "Weird habits that actually work" (@lauriemarbas, 854x, 7.7k likes)
**Structure:** a list title, then each habit as one plain line plus a funny or vivid reason.

> Weird journaling habits that actually work.
>
> Write about not knowing what to write. It feels stupid. Three lines in, you'll know exactly what's bothering you.
>
> Journal like Arthur from Red Dead Redemption 2. Sketch what you saw today. Jot what happened. Cowboy philosopher energy.
>
> Write your complaints down instead of saying them out loud. Once they're on the page, they stop being yours.
>
> Carry a pocket notebook and a pencil. Your best thoughts show up in the queue at the supermarket, not at your desk.

## 3. Template: "Weekend doing nothing" reframe (@theslowreturn, 611x, 21.9k likes)
**Structure:** "I love when someone says X…" then the irony, then a quiet one-line reframe.

> I love when someone says they don't have time to journal and then tells me about the three hours they lost to their phone yesterday.
>
> Journaling can be one sentence. Ten seconds. No screen.
>
> We don't lack time. We've just become very strange about what we count as worth our time.

## 4. Template: "Succeed more than 95% of people" (@itscoachgoodman, 429x)
**Structure:** a bold promise, then 2–3 almost insultingly simple bullets.

> How to think more clearly than 95% of people:
>
> Write one sentence a day.
>
> By hand.

## 5. Template: rhetorical one-liner (@elevatestart, 262x, 17.9k likes)
**Structure:** one sentence, a question built on a list of 3–4 stakes.

> How can you feel lost when there's a page waiting to hold your thoughts, untangle your confusion, and show you where you've been going all along?

## 6. Template: "X, not Y" stack (@sebastianmorino, 181x)
**Structure:** parallel lines of "do this — not that", then a punchy identity closer.

> Write 1 sentence, not 3 pages.
>
> Use a pen, not an app.
>
> Write what you feel, not what sounds smart.
>
> Ask 1 question, not 10.
>
> Write daily, not when you're "inspired."
>
> You're building a habit, not a masterpiece.

## 7. Template: antithesis couplet (@elevatestart, 144x, 9.9k likes)
**Structure:** two mirrored sentences that flip one variable.

> The problem with journaling is that one day's entry looks pointless.
>
> The problem with not journaling is that ten years can look pointless too.

## 8. Template: "Want X? Stop Y. Start Z." (@elevatedpath, 80x)
**Structure:** a question hook, then stop/start, then a teased list.

> Want to stop feeling scattered?
>
> Stop starting new things.
>
> Start writing down the old ones.
>
> 4 one-sentence journal prompts that turn chaos into direction:
>
> 1. What am I avoiding right now?
> 2. What did I drop, and why?
> 3. What keeps pulling me back?
> 4. If I could only keep one thread, which one?

## 9. Template: "Could replace an MBA" authority list (@natanmohart, 73x)
**Structure:** a contrarian title, big names, then "you don't need X to Y."

> 5 journals that could replace a philosophy degree.
>
> Marcus Aurelius — *Meditations* (literally his private journal)
> Leonardo da Vinci — notebooks of sketches, theories, and feelings
> Charles Darwin — field notes and a personal diary
> Ryan Holiday — a lifetime of notecards
> Yours — the one you haven't started yet
>
> You don't need a library to think like the greats. You need a pen and one sentence a day.

## 10. Template: Naval-style compressed creed (@navalsarchive, 6.7k likes)
**Structure:** clipped verb lists, one directive, then a calm closer.

> Wake, write, reflect, notice, release.
>
> Doubt it, question it, write it down, read it back.
>
> One sentence. By hand. Every day.
>
> Skip the app, the course, the new hobby, the endless scroll.
>
> Relax. The pieces are already on the table.
