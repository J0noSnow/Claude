The same formula drops from 22.9k likes in February to 1.7k in October. I read that as format fatigue, but I can't prove the cause.

**The shape of one promo note** (May, June, July, August, September):
1. **Hook = a feeling about the moment, said as a fact.** "may always feels like possibility..." / "june always feels like the month where..." Her own tag for it is "identity stake". The reader sees themselves before they see an offer.
2. **Name the pain in her own words.** "47 open tabs", "hovering just outside your life", "stop living entirely in your head".
3. **"I made" + an exact number + the outcome.** "31 prompts to help you stop circling your life in your head."
4. **Contrast with the phone.** "something softer to reach for than your phone." The essay titles repeat it: "(instead of doomscrolling)".
5. **A soft ask, no hard sell.** "it's here if you want something to keep you company." Ends with "<3".
6. **Reply bait that creates a gap.** "day 1 is 👀", "i need your day 1 answer", "if anyone gets to day 30 let me know". It invites a reply under the note, which is worth more than a like.
7. **The essay rides along as a card** with a clear title and a one-line benefit subtitle. The note carries the feeling. The card carries the link.
8. **Look and tone:** lowercase, warm, collage image, one emoji.

**What underperforms:** her promo for a craft essay ("you do not need a more interesting, carrie bradshaw-esque life to write personal essays...") got 65 likes (0.5x). Promos about the reader's inner life beat promos about craft.

**What I kept and changed:**
- Kept: the number, the contrast with the phone, the reply bait, the soft ask, the "feeling as fact" hook, and the essay card.
- Changed: your voice (sentence case, dry, a little self-deprecating), no copied phrasing, no em dashes. I also kept your title's curiosity gap in posts 1 and 2 by not naming the phrase.

## 1. The month hook
**Pattern:** feeling about the moment as the hook + "I made" + number + phone contrast + reply bait

> October is a good month to stop shrinking yourself.
>
> I wrote 31 journal prompts about the one phrase multi-passionate people use to downplay everything they can do, and why it hides which way we're really heading.
>
> It's here if you want something to reach for that isn't your phone. Day 1 is a little uncomfortable 👀

## 2. The piano confession
**Pattern:** confession that creates a gap + reply bait (the phrase stays hidden, like your title)

> I know one song on piano. I will never call myself a pianist.
>
> I have 3k subscribers and over a year of essays, and I still flinch when someone says "writer."
>
> There's a phrase I reach for every single time. This month's prompts are about catching it.
>
> What do you say when someone asks what you do? Tell me below.

## 3. Three prompts as the note
**Pattern:** the value is in the note, the essay holds the rest (her "five letters" and "10 gentle invitations" notes)

> 3 journal prompts for anyone who downplays what they can do:
>
> 1. What would you call yourself if no one could ask a follow-up question?
> 2. Which interest do you keep casual so you never have to find out if you're any good?
> 3. Who were you protecting the last time you called something a hobby: them, or you?
>
> The other 28 are in this month's essay.

## 4. The five-stage arc
**Pattern:** a clear structure and a low barrier to start (her "no pressure" framing)

> 31 days. One phrase. Five stages.
>
> Hear it. Behind it. Under it. Past it. Say it differently.
>
> First you notice where you say it. Then what it protects you from. Then the skill that's actually there. Then which way you're already leaning. Then you replace it.
>
> No daily commitment. Skip days, double up. Ten minutes and a pen is enough.
>
> October's prompts are here.

## 5. The rebrand joke
**Pattern:** behind-the-scenes note (her milestone and "12 months ago" notes did well) + the essay's other two announcements

> If I got a dollar every time I rebranded myself, I'd be a hundredaire.
>
> David Bowie reinvented himself over and over before he found his path, so I'm calling it research.
>
> The new name is permanent: Jono's Journal. Everything I make starts in the journal anyway. There's also a chat now for people who refuse to pick one thing.
>
> The details and October's 31 prompts are in this month's essay.
