## Diagnosis of entry 2 (my analysis, to check against the original article)
**Root cause:** the playbook was written in two compression steps (letter -> three-line note -> lesson), so the step-by-step detail of exercises was lost at the first step. It likely affects other exercises, not just this one.

**What Lesson 1.2 has:** the skeleton (two anchor dots, 2-3 territories, 3-5 sub-topics, 3-5 ideas per sub-topic, draw connections, "magnet not checklist").

**What it is missing from the original article (to restore in own words in v2.0):**
- The three reasons people follow you, and why the third (belief you can change their life) drives the mission.
- The two-sheet method: sheet 1 is the mission page, sheet 2 is the map; do it on paper.
- Sheet 1 layout and its two question sets: a line down the centre, anti-vision on one side and vision on the other, each with four specific prompts (the lowest point, the worst version of yourself, what you do to avoid it, the skills and mindset required, why). The 15-minute timebox, keeping the page beside you, and adding to it as you read.
- "The best brands have an enemy supporters can rally against."
- That the vision and anti-vision *are* the niche, and the audience is people one or two steps behind you. The interview-style AI prompt for brainstorming this (the "life reset map") is a separate paid letter.
- Map construction details: dots at the far left and right, territories spaced evenly in between, and the map metaphor's parts (roads, obstacles, attractions, legend, landscape).
- Why territories must be broad (scrollers are not there to learn; it gives the mind room) with worked examples of narrowing up to a broad label, and the rules for fewer than three topics, no topic yet, and choosing at the intersection of what you care about and what can earn.
- Sub-topic examples for several domains, and the option to ask AI for a list to choose from.
- The eighth idea type ("anything that moves the reader one step toward the right-hand dot") and the "map, not the territory" caveat: drop it if it overwhelms.
- The connecting-lines exercise with an example, and its limit: infinite combinations, but not all perform.
