## 1. "Growing on social media is easy, actually" (2026-05-07)
Gist: Audience growth reduces to two levers: ride proven topics from your own angle, and daily friendly outreach to peers.
- [A] Lever 1: people come to platforms for an opinion on a topic they already like, not for novelty. Take an outlier title/topic that has worked and fill it with your own content. DEMO: his 2019 tweet hook "how to get ahead of 99% of people" became a 2023 video (1.7M views); then others (Manson 4.7M, Hormozi 1.7M, Bilyeu retitle 1.5M) reused the same title with different content. Also DEMO'd another creator's first two videos at 144k/254k views using stock topics.
- [A][I] Packaging (title/hook/thumbnail) borrowed; substance original. Title templates recur ("how to become dangerously X"). OPINION: topics are not ownable.
- [A] Research first, plan ideas second, write third; when growth stalls, slot in a validated-topic piece to pull eyes toward more esoteric ideas. DEMO (his habit).
- [A] Lever 2, "non-needy networking": ~30 min/day in replies/DMs. Other people's audiences are the source of followers. 7 steps: pick someone you actually want to talk to (near your follower size at first); send casual, honest praise (echo their own post, text-like tone, no expectation); show interest in their projects; be useful (send resources that remind you of them); optionally get on a call/voice notes; keep sending relevant things; then ask for a share, with a post written so they would want to share it. Bonus: show up in their replies first so DM isn't cold. Claims it's half a beginner's job. DEMO (did Zoom calls early).
- [A] Social media is a skill with mechanics "like a video game"; beginners fail by treating it as luck. OPINION.
- [I] Background story: ~12 failed ventures (digital art, dropshipping, agencies, photography) each supplied a skill later reused (brand aesthetics, copywriting, networking, funnel design).
- Exercises: take your best-performing old post/title and rewrite it from a different angle; DM 3 writers a day with specific praise + one useful link; list 10 outlier titles in your niche.
Form: Opens with blunt claim ("easy, actually"), personal credibility backstory, "2 levers" frame, numbered 7-step how-to with sample DM scripts, mid-letter product pitch (Eden, 50% off) flagged as "sales pitch over," ends asking for a like. ~3,000 words. Header-heavy, short lines, casual swearing. CTA: product plus like/subscribe.

## 2. "The unpleasant truth: AI is coming for your job" (2025-05-13)
Gist: Become AI-first by turning your own processes into prompts, then compete on mastery, attention and a small true-fan base.
- [I] Hook: stacked alarming social-proof (tweet quoting AI vs 85%/99.9% of humans, Fiverr CEO memo, other CEOs) -> "Scared yet?" -> promise of optimism ("most exciting time to be alive"). Fear-then-reframe pattern.
- [I] Named concepts: "abstract up a layer" (labor -> mind), "mastery and meaning economy," "autopilot to autocomplete," "authenticity at scale," "you are the niche." Borrows ideas from other writers (Signull, Cosmos Institute) and credits them.
- [N][A] Practical routine (DEMO): "practice automating yourself out of work" -- write the full process, turn each piece into a prompt, test, refine to ~90%, store the prompt. He keeps a prompt library (title generator for newsletters; a prompt that writes prompts). OPINION: output quality = prompt quality.
- [A] Let AI do what you dislike (marketing, admin, sales) but don't outsource the writing you care about; AI-written text "isn't from my mind."
- [L] Economics of small audience: ~500 people at $10/month = $5k/mo (more with annual); $50-150 products; $5k client services; spin-off books/software. Offer stack = recurring + high ticket + low ticket. "1,000 true fans" is even less. DEMO-ish math, OPINION on feasibility.
- [A] Safety net = a body of work impossible to ignore; build "a world people can explore" rather than a sales funnel.
- Exercise: document one weekly task as a step-by-step prompt; calculate your own 500-subscriber math.
Form: Hook stats, three section "claims" numbered, cites other writers with block quotes, embedded screenshots, soft paywall break ("paid tier price increases tomorrow"), ends with "Staying the same is the enemy of a good life." Multiple CTAs in footer: paid tier (+30% price rise urgency), free weekly Saturday list (175k+ subscribers), Kortex tool.

## 3. "You can learn anything in 2 weeks" (2025-07-17)

- [I] Reframe: don't learn "guitar," learn one song; don't learn Photoshop, learn to remove a tree background. Skills = stacks of techniques. Progressive overload analogy.
- [C] Implication for products: teach techniques tied to results, not tool tours (implicit).
- [L] Money as a learnable skill: purpose -> need product + customer -> look up technique for finding idea (links his "mega guide" paid-tier piece) -> build without over-validating -> pick one traffic channel -> stack/test.
- [I] Named tool: "tactical stress" = engineered deadline. DEMO: learned After Effects in ~2 weeks by filling a weekly YouTube storyboard with animations he couldn't yet make. (Elsewhere defines it as public launches yearly.)
- [N] Embedded "subscribe if you like this" mid-letter; links to prior letters; ends with a "continue reading" list.
Exercise: choose a skill, define a single deliverable, schedule a public deadline for it.
Form: Declarative headline-as-first-line, caveat to defuse objection ("I don't mean master"), H3s with two-word names, strong analogies (gym), closes with "That, my friends, is how you learn." ~1,700 words, short, no hard sell.

## 4. "The Micro Education Business Model (How to Monetize Your Knowledge)" (undated, ~2023)
Gist: Core of his system: newsletter -> medium-form thread -> short-form posts, then monetize via services/products/sponsors.
- [N] Start a newsletter now precisely because nobody is watching; long form builds authority, you own the list, it's where you sell. DEMO/OPINION.
- [N][A] Content ecosystem (DEMO): write the long newsletter first; reuse as YouTube script and blog post; condense to 10-15 tweet thread (becomes IG/LinkedIn carousel); break thread into standalone tweets. Short form = testing ground; most newsletter ideas come from tweets that already performed.
- [I] Idea hunting: sort favourite creators by most popular, study headlines on Medium, read books for a novel idea, study top tweets (his favourite). Idea quality beats production polish. Learn copy frameworks (PAS, AIDA, PASTOR) as "training wheels."
- [I] Three pillars of a creator: inspiration, education, entertainment; he uses personal experience / philosophical musings / hard advice. "You are the niche." "Mental real estate." Write for impact, income follows.
- [L] Monetization ladder (his tweet): study interest -> service business -> grow audience -> work for free -> develop process -> raise prices -> repeat -> productize. Other examples cited: Shaan Puri $50k/mo sponsorships at 100k subs; adsense vs sponsorships (Chewning); digital product adds a third stream. "Give value, build distribution, ask to get paid."
- [C] Defends info products as meaningful; he credits cheap courses for his path.
- Philosophical aside: people replicate "information in consciousness" (a conceptual hook for why teaching matters).
Form: Personal opening about starting out, myth-busting H2, numbered 3-point model, bullet-heavy, product plugs (Digital Economics, 2 Hour Writer, TweetHunter, Keyframe), ends "Enjoy your week."
