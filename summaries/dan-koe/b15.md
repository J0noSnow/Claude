# Batch b15 notes (13 Dan Koe letters). Tags: [A] free writing/audience, [N] newsletter craft, [L] ladder/offers/launch, [C] courses/products, [I] ideas/philosophy. D=DEMO, O=OPINION.

Cross-batch patterns: Letters open with a blunt claim or reader-callout, use H2/H3 sections with an epigraph quote (Fuller, Einstein, Krishnamurti, De Mello, Deida), numbered steps, short one-sentence paragraphs, sign off "– Dan". CTA is a soft plug at the end or a mid-letter "subtle reminder" box (product/bootcamp/challenge). Almost every letter is built on the same 4-5 recurring concepts: anti-vision/vision, goals as filter, identity change, skill-tree/levels of development, creation as meaning. Old letters (2022-2024, thedankoe.com, undated in files) are more spiritual/Alan Watts; 2025-26 (Substack) are more structured, framework/diagram-led.

## 1. "If you want to be happy, be a loser" (2025-06-12)
Gist: Reject both Western "doing" and Eastern "being" in favour of "becoming"; redefine success as progress + contribution + emotional mastery, via four steps (master yourself, true education, belonging, contribute).
- Hook: provocative imperative opening with 3 short "do this" lines (let parents think you're stupid, start the business...). Then a "reframe the word" move (happiness is a state; use "success" instead). Mid-letter "Subtle reminder" box promoting a 30-day personal brand challenge starting in 4 days; repeated as a closing CTA with a one-line bridge ("personal branding encapsulates everything we've discussed"). [L] DEMO: cohort/challenge sold with a countdown, plugged twice (top and bottom) inside a philosophical essay.
- [I] Framework naming: borrows Wilber's 4 quadrants, renames them "4 dimensions of reality". Pattern: take an existing model, rename, apply to a practical life question. Also "developmental lines" = skill-tree analogy (generated list via AI, he says so openly).
- [I] Argument pattern: show two opposing camps (West/East), say both reject half of reality, offer an integration ("both/and"). Epigraph from Krishnamurti. Section titles are the thesis ("The key to success is being a useful loser").
- [C]/[A] Learning method (DEMO of his own philosophy): self-generated goal -> core skills -> fundamentals -> 40+ hrs experimenting with techniques -> note what works -> reflect. "Technique stacking" not "learning Photoshop."
- [A] Opinion: the internet is a feedback loop; if nobody cares, treat as problem -> hypothesis -> experiment. Get paid to be yourself = become valuable + understand what others want. Find the overlap of what you care about and what others care about. O.
- [A] Use the For You page sparingly to discover people, then follow/save their feeds ("reduce randomness"). O.
- Exercise: write your own definition of success in one sentence, then check it covers progress, contribution, emotional regulation.
- Form: ~1,900 words, 4 numbered sections, diagrams as images.

## 2. "How to think like a strategic genius (5D thinking)" (2026-01-27)
Gist: Smart people think badly because they stop thinking when ideas threaten identity; build up from lines -> levels -> altitude -> 4 dimensions -> time (5th).
- [I] Hook: "Your ability to think determines your life... especially if you're smart, you're most likely to think like an idiot." Promise: "if you read carefully you'll experience thinking on a new level." Explicitly says he'll skip first-principles/systems thinking since ChatGPT can teach those.
- [I] Technique: define a thing by its opposite first (what stupid thinking is: one-dimensional, reductionist, tribal, unquestioning), then derive a definition of genius. Knowing = horizontal, understanding = vertical.
- [I] Escalating-dimension structure (1D -> 5D) is itself the hook and the organizing device; he even admits the "5th dimension" is made up for the sake of the gimmick and calls it practice. DEMO of naming/gamifying an idea.
- Frameworks: lines (domains), levels (instinctual, conformist, individualist, synthesist, generative), altitude (average of levels), 4 quadrants as perspectives for any problem, then time/history via "transcend and include." Applied example: a problem ("world is becoming meaningless") examined from each quadrant as 4 questions. DEMO of how to apply a model.
- [I] Core claim: identity is what stops thinking; when threatened, sit with it. Closing line is a short imperative aphorism.
- Skill-tree analogy recurs (cross-letter motif).
- [A]/[N] CTA is minimal: a single "Subscribe now" button mid-letter, no product pitch. Pure authority/value letter.
- Exercise: pick one current problem, write one question from each of the 4 perspectives.
- Form: ~2,300 words, epigraphs (Fuller, Einstein, Krishnamurti), 5 H3 sections, bolded key terms.

## 3. "You need to be delusional if you want to succeed" (2026-09-12)
Gist: Realistic goals keep you average; set an impossible goal with an impossible timeline, subtract everything unrelated, then find the highest-leverage path.
- [N] Hook: "Tell me if any of these sound like you" with 3 numbered reader-recognition scenarios, each with a consequence ("give it 5 years..."). Then promises the letter fixes it and teases a series ("next few letters"). Ends with a next-letter teaser (how to add gravity to goals). Good serial/retention device. DEMO.
- [I] Argument pattern: reframe goal as a filter/point of view, not a destination (tied to selective attention). Contrasts two characters (Tommy vs Johnny). Uses Steve Jobs' reality distortion field as the proof story. Epigraph stack (Musk, Maltz, De Mello).
- [I] 3 steps: 1) install the point of view (goal too big + timeline too short; test: could current approach reach it? if yes, raise it; must be singular and self-chosen), 2) set non-negotiable "don't do" standards (audit habits, clients, obligations), 3) pick the highest-leverage path.
- [L] DEMO with numbers: his first $100k month goal when he was a freelancer under $100k/yr with about 1,000 followers. Tactics: treat posts as distribution for one offer rather than as the product; borrow distribution by trading value with an account of about 50,000 followers who shares your posts to their audience. This is the most concrete ladder tactic in the batch.
- [A] OPINION: "post consistently and hope" does not work for a delusional goal; need force multipliers (offer + borrowed audience).
- [C] CTA: bootcamp bundled with his Eden product; invites free signup to get notified of a "get paid for your knowledge" update. Soft, placed after the personal story (story -> CTA).
- Exercise: write a goal that fails the "current approach" test, then list 5 things you will stop doing.
- Form: ~1,600 words, notebook-style steps ("pull out a notebook"), short punchy paragraphs.

## 4. "Disappear and come back unrecognizable (12 rules to change your life)" (undated, thedankoe.com ~2024)
Gist: A 12-part life-design chain from anti-vision to experimentation: Anti-Vision, Vision, Mission, Standards, Goals, Projects, Education, Limitations, Levers, Challenge, Curiosity, Experimentation.
- [I] Hook: personal claim that "I don't know what I want" really means "I don't want the work". Then lists what he doesn't want (hated job, body, partner, mind) and derives what to do from it. DEMO of anti-vision method: start from what you reject.
- [I] Named framework ("The 12 Rules of Creation") presented as a universal pattern that also maps onto marketing, stories, games, company structure. Pattern: one-word headings + 2-5 line explanation each, so each rule is memorable and quotable.
- Concrete tactics: keep a running note of unwanted experiences; 30-minute "minimum viable vision" iterated; goals by decade/year/month/week/day ("stubborn with vision, loose with details"); build the project first, learn gaps after; "limitations" = what you refuse to sacrifice (anti-goal, forces creativity); daily priority "levers" (writing is his main lever); flow = challenge/skill match; experiment with several techniques and make your own process. Mostly O except his own anti-vision and 7 failed businesses before success (D, one line).
- [A] Mention: writing on social media as leverage; writing is the main lever of his creator business.
- [C]/[L] CTA: inline product mentions for writing course and community in the "Levers" section (contextual plug, not a closing pitch).
- Decision rule: zoom out daily on what you don't want; ask "will this benefit the future I'm creating?"
- Exercise: 30 minutes writing a minimum viable vision plus an anti-vision list.
- Form: ~1,500 words, very scannable, aphoristic; closes without a hard CTA. Image-led.

## 5. "How to actually make $1 million with AI" (undated, ~2025 Apr)
Gist: AI doesn't remove the need for traffic, offer, landing page, copy; he demos chaining prompt flows inside his own product (Kortex) for brand strategy -> avatar -> offer -> landing page -> content.
- [L] Hook: calls out the clickbait reader ("I bet you clicked for a quick way to make money") then deflates it with a checklist of what you still need (traffic, content, offer, avatar, landing page, copy). Honest-contrarian hook that sets up a sale. DEMO.
- [L] Numbers math (DEMO of back-solving a goal): $1M/yr = ~$83k/month = ~$2.8k/day; paths: 18 sales/day of a $150 product, 111/day of $25 subs, a $5k client every other day, a $10k client every 4 days. At 2.5% landing page conversion need about 720 visitors/day for 18 sales. Compare to 500k-1M monthly social impressions or 10-50k views per video, so 720 clicks is a small share. Great template for a "reverse-engineer your revenue goal" post.
- [L] Ladder/process: brand strategy interview -> customer avatar -> "irresistible offer" blueprint (delivery format, big idea, big problem/pain points, quantifiable outcome, unique mechanism, features/benefits, Hormozi value equation) -> text-only landing page (headline variants) hosted on Stan Store -> posts/scripts/emails. He claims 5 years and 10+ products launched. Says text-only pages often convert best (O/D mix). Warns: test and iterate; keep conversing with AI.
- [N] AI-use tip: use it as a conversational partner, iterate (ask why it isn't converting, regenerate 10 headlines).
- [A] Reason for social: free, test content before ads, audience = leverage.
- Personal stance: he writes, outsources marketing he dislikes (O).
- Pure product tutorial / soft pitch; CTAs: Kortex signup, affiliate program, free PDF book Purpose & Profit.
- Exercise: do the $1M division with your own price and conversion rate; list what you'd need in traffic.
- Form: ~1,300 words, screenshot-led walkthrough, step headings named after funnel stages.

## 6. "How to learn anything 10x faster than anyone with AI" (undated, ~Mar 2025)
Gist: Learn by building a project tied to your ideal life, struggle first, learn on demand, and write in public weekly to reflect on what you learn.
- [I]/[C] Hook: "Most people don't know how to learn... 6 months later nothing to show." Promise "very practical letter, have notes open." Learn-by-project flow: (1) map ideal life with 10+ bullets each for want / don't want (learning filter), (2) outline a project, (3) start with what you know, expose gaps, search/AI on demand, (3.5) Zeigarnik trick: do easy pre-tasks to make starting easy, (4) study regimen. - [N] DEMO of his newsletter process: outlines the week's letter at the start of the week, which creates a frame of reference; captures ideas during walks/conversations into the draft on his phone; writes in dedicated work blocks. A very reusable workflow.
- [A] Writing as public journal: treat social media as a place to put work in front of people, not "being a content creator"; the Feynman technique + protege effect (teaching exposes gaps). Starter plan (O): weekly newsletter summarizing what you learned (teach, don't dump), posts on X/Threads/LinkedIn, extra 30-60 min morning writing block, ask AI for hook help. - [I] Project start checklist: save 3-5 inspiration examples, break down their structure, outline sections/milestones.
- [C] CTA: writers bootcamp starting "in 3 days" (urgency stated in last section), plus free Simple Life Reset doc as lead magnet.
- Exercise: pick a project, list 3-5 reference pieces and write each one's structure in bullets.

## 10. "The best online business model to make $1 million in 2023" (undated, ~Mar 2023)
Gist: Real business = offer + traffic; personal brand is the universal traffic source; start with a minimum viable offer (1 skill/interest) as freelance/coaching/tutoring at $500-1000, then cohort, then self-paced course.
- [L] Hook: mocks clickbait topics ("best side hustles 2023") then promises the big picture. Contrarian stance: quick-money chasers have 3-6 months before pivoting.
- [L] Highest-density letter for ladder tactics:

  - Minimum viable offer: one skill (email, web design, copywriting) or interest (fitness etc.) as freelance, coaching, consulting, tutoring. Tutoring as the beginner choice. Marketing = Goal (where they want to be) + Path + Problem; sell a transformation.
  - Pack of 4 weekly calls, weekly action items, text/chat access; define point A and point B and outline path. Price: start at $500, rise to $1,000 after a few clients, $2,500+ later. Rationale: easier to make one $1,000 sale than ten $100 sales; no audience yet for course sales; unknown what gets results.
  - Free calls for fellow creators for testimonials that appreciate in value as they grow ("testimonials from creators are appreciating assets"). DEMO/O.
  - Ladder: 1-1 offer -> cohort course fueled by audience -> self-paced course. His own route: personal brand + digital products + iteration; took 3 years to $1M with some prior experience (DEMO).
- [A] Personal brand lets you pivot topics; you "become the niche."
- Exercise: write a 4-call pack: point A, point B, 4 weekly outlines and action items, price.

## 12. "The rise of the generalist (how to thrive with multiple interests)" (undated, ~May 2024)
Gist: In a "second renaissance" generalists win; build a general audience framed around one big goal, find signal, create a digital asset, and launch a free plus paid product every 3-6 months.
- [A] Concrete audience playbook (the best in this batch):
  - Choose a big goal (financial freedom, fit body, etc.); list your skills/interests/beliefs relevant; frame everything through that goal. Bigger goal -> bigger audience. Example post: emotional management steps with a closing line tying to money/relationships. DEMO of his post formula: idea list -> focus on how/why -> write -> filter through the big goal.
  - "Make noise, find signal": post about all interests, treat poor posts as data; review weekly, double down on winners: turn into thread/carousel/newsletter/video, rewrite from multiple angles, bake into routine. O with partial DEMO.
  - Why generalist: algorithm/reposts launch you into diverse audiences; people on social media are there to be entertained, so persuade them of the importance of your topic. States he'd rather have 100k diverse followers than 10k niche.
- [L]/[C] Digital asset: when a topic keeps working, make a free product or pinned thread containing almost everything you know about it (so you stop repeating yourself and can explore new ideas) which feeds the email list. Then portfolio of income: courses, cohorts, templates, systems. Cadence: every 3-6 months launch at least one free and one paid product. Examples: Zuby (rapper/fitness/commentary), his own planner and a bad first ebook that led to a real book. Waits for cash flow before capital-heavy products (D).
- [I] Concept coining: "failure stacking" instead of skill stacking; his own failures (dropshipping, e-commerce, SEO, digital art) each taught a skill (DEMO story). "Most profitable niche is you." Be top 25% in a few areas.
- CTAs: free course, writing course, and a monetization course with a launch date ("early bird price increases June 3").
- Form: ~2,600 words, list-based problems then solutions, images of diagrams.

## Cross-letter takeaways for Jono's Journal
1. Hook types seen: reader-callout list ("tell me if any of these sound like you"), anti-hook ("this won't be a hack"), contrarian thesis, bluntly diagnosing the clickbait reader, escalating-dimension gimmick.
2. Idea generation: take an established model (Wilber, Csikszentmihalyi, skill tree, flow), rename it, and apply to a practical problem; build "X vs Y" pairs; define by negation (anti-vision, anti-goal, "what stupid thinking is"); recycle five concepts across letters.
3. Funnel tactics that were demonstrated: free letter -> challenge/bootcamp with countdown plugs (top and bottom); low-ticket membership ($5); free PDF/doc lead magnets; free product to collect emails; 4-call coaching pack at $500-1000 -> cohort -> self-paced course; launch a free + paid product every 3-6 months; borrow audience by trading value with a larger account.
4. Newsletter workflow: outline early in the week, collect ideas during walks, write in a dedicated block; review post performance weekly and expand winners into longer pieces.
5. Plugs are placed contextually (right where the related claim is made) rather than only at the end; cross-linking to older letters is constant.
