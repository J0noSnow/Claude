## 3 Drills to master short-form posts (2025-05-14) [A][I]
- He says he can't teach a style, only practice routines; each drill = 10 posts, so one sitting yields a few weeks of content.
- Drill 1: list 10 deliberately mediocre ideas; take 10 saved post structures; for each, first write why the structure works (hook opens a loop, novel truth, implied before/after, polarizing filter), then rewrite one of your ideas in it without copying hooks. Meant to be hard.
- Drill 2: consume something long (book/video/podcast), force yourself to extract 10 post ideas, rewrite them without structure templates. Works better when you have a mission or 2-3 topic pillars; the "this is writable" feeling builds over time.
- Drill 3: pick 5-10 fast-growing creators in your topics; study their most popular posts/videos; note repeated ideas; rewrite validated ideas through your own take.
- Claims: most creators in a topic circle the same 5-10 ideas; growth is slow then bursts (DEMO: his YouTube went from about 7k to 300k in 2-3 months after about a year of consistency); smart creators befriend people slightly ahead of them.

## How to Build a World / 2-hour content ecosystem 2.0 (2025-05-10) [A][N][L]
- Frame: build a body of work like a fictional universe: long-form = films, short-form = spinoffs, products = merchandise; make it bingeable so viewers become fans become superfans. Don't write unique content per platform; make one strong piece and spread it widely.
- Level 1 loop: idea with potential -> newsletter -> product link inside -> spin-off posts -> link the newsletter under one post daily -> reuse best posts as new ideas. One long-form home (public-link newsletter) plus one writing-based short-form platform.
- Level 2 (only after level 1 works): newsletter as video script; podcast from the video; embed video in the public newsletter page; best posts become images or short-video scripts; promote old newsletters daily.
- Newsletter = lead magnet and "stealth sales page": link it under posts instead of using a bare opt-in page; promote a product at least once inside it; long form builds more trust than short form. Linking mechanics differ per platform (comment links, DM automations, bio link, story links).
- Writing posts from a newsletter: don't paste excerpts; rewrite each idea so it stands alone with its own hook; about 7 posts per weekly newsletter, one for each day to link under.
- Idea gathering routine (DEMO): walks with audiobooks/long videos, a curated feed, two note types (newsletter ideas, standalone post ideas); then writing is just clarifying. Beginners: pull popular YouTube titles from relevant channels as angles; spot outlier posts.
- Schedule (2 hours/day): newsletter 60 min on 5 days (outline one day, post another), 30 min posts, 30 min engagement + linking; later swap days for filming/editing. Maintenance mode is year-round; during a launch, focus on the launch for 1-2 months.

## Full guide: how I write about deep ideas and still go viral (2025-05-06) [N][I][A]
- Claim of scale: over 175k newsletter subscribers and over 1M YouTube subscribers (his statement).
- Topic choice = intersection of what you love and what people have shown they want; position a niche idea under a validated angle. Method (DEMO): find 3-5 channels, sort by popular, note top titles, fit your idea to one angle; example: tying a developmental-psychology idea to an "ahead of 99% of people" angle. Titles from video transfer to newsletter headlines.
- Objection handled: ignoring what people want means nobody reads; writing is a two-player game.
- Frameworks help; most readers are beginners who are scrolling for entertainment, so hook first, then guide through a transformation.
- Ideation checklist before drafting: the big problem, desired outcome, aha insight, unique process, a novel concept, optional timeframe.
- Write for your past self; define obscure terms in plain language with examples, even if the piece gets long; this is why he says you don't need a traditional niche.
- Structure: hook of 200-500 words attacking "the enemy" (past self, common view, average behavior) and setting up the transformation with an "if you..." promise; 2-3 key points for deep topics (each: quote, definition, plain explanation, example, application, action) or many for practical lists.
- Getting unstuck: problem, quote, explanation, example, metaphor, statistic, goal ("if you want..."), action steps.
