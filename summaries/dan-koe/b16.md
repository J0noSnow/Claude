## 1. The Death of Thoughtful Creation (& How to Get Ahead of Everyone Else) | 2025-06-19
Gist: AI makes output cheap, so value shifts to taste, story and curation; commit 6-12 months to building a real project, a personal brand and a product.
- [I] Core frame: machines win on speed/accuracy/utility, humans on "meaning, play, signal". Contrast pairs he uses repeatedly: factories vs gardeners, barista vs local coffee shop owner, bullet summary vs handwritten vows. OPINION
- [I] Idea-naming: "leisure economy", "curation over creation", "taste". Taste = restraint + discernment + what you let into your mind; taste cannot be outsourced, only built by making things. OPINION
- [A] Taste grows via iteration; he says his first newsletter now looks awful to him. Quitting after two weeks means never developing taste. DEMO (anecdote)
- [A][I] Do not follow passion; cultivate it by doing something with care long enough. Set 1 hour a day for craftsmanship on a project. OPINION
- [A][L] Three-step path: (1) personal brand with story front and centre, published in public; (2) curate inputs so outputs carry signal; (3) turn the project into a product, since without one you end up selling someone else's. 6-12 months, 2-4 hrs/day. OPINION
- [I] Evidence style: history sweep (plow surplus -> Axial age philosophy; 80% self-employed then vs ~10% now), plus epigraph quotes (Naval, Chris Paik).
Exercises: Write a one-paragraph "what only I could say because of my story" note; pick one project and log 1 hour daily for 30 days.
Form: Opens with a blockquote claim, then a "most people do this wrong" turn; H2 sections with escalating "how" questions (admits "sounds nice, but how?"), H3 action steps. Long (~2,000 words). Ends without hard CTA, just "Good luck", with a small "latest paid post" plug (One Person Business Launchpad) mid-way and related-reading list. Short one-line paragraphs for rhythm; triplets ("Consuming less. Producing less. Doing less.").
