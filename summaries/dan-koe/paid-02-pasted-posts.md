# Notes on 6 pasted paid letters (Koe). Own-words paraphrase. Tags: [A] audience [N] newsletter [L] ladder/launch [C] courses/products [I] ideas/philosophy. DEMO = he says he did it; OPINION = claim.

## 1. Persuasive landing pages with AI or without (2025-05-21) [L][C]
- Copywriting = clearly communicating the offer. Lazy route: train an AI on copywriting principles, feed it product details, get a first draft, host on a no-design tool. (DEMO: his own prompt build.)
- Prompt-building method: (1) get a reasoning model to produce a detailed copywriting guide from a well-known book; (2) ask the model what info it would need from you; (3) combine both into a meta-prompt in two phases: gather context, then write. Output quality depends on input quality; expect a first draft only.
- Framework: PASTOR = Problem, Amplify, Story, Testimony, Offer, Response.
- Page order: eyebrow, headline, subheadline, optional top proof, lead (pain list), body (story + mechanism), offer intro (feature then benefit), proof, guarantee, call to action, FAQ, objections. Keep top sections fast; about 600-800 words; creators need less long copy because audience trust does work.
- Headline formula: [desired outcome] without [main pain/inconvenience]. Generate 10-20 headlines, pick one.
- Lead uses "bullet spray" of 5-6 qualifying pain points. Body shares a short story then the mechanism, giving away enough to create want. Call to action offers two paths: buy, or keep figuring it out alone, without pressure.
- If a product doesn't sell, the usual cause is promotion and content, not the page. Use a simple host (Stan, or Carrd for custom); don't delay selling for design.
- Writing tips: short sentences, write to one person, start sections with problem/idea/stat/quote, draft without judging then edit as the customer.

## 2. How to launch a product: simple 3-week strategy (2025-05-25) [L]
- Launch = biggest revenue moment; assumes daily short-form and a weekly long-form piece. Bake in scarcity (enrollment closing, price rising, bonuses ending). Pick date at least 3 weeks out and narrow content to the product topic. (DEMO: his routine for years.)
- Week 1 soft: mention in newsletter near the top and in the P.S.; under the day's best short post (after 1-2 hours) link the newsletter, not the product.
- Week 2 harder: list problems the product solves; under best post each day plug the product plus the deadline using a template: "If you struggle with A, B, C, [product] closes [date]." Use auto-DM tools on platforms where replies aren't seen.
- Week 3: repeat; final 3 days post a standalone promotion each day; last day send two emails (a full "final day" email, then a short reminder 4-6 hours before close) and a last-call post with details in replies, deleted after.
- Sales: slow week 1, picks up week 2, surge week 3 and the last 2 days. Network reposts matter; claims a 1,000-follower account with a strong network can out-earn a 50,000 one.

## 3. One-Person Business Launchpad course (2025-06-10) [L][C][A][N][I]
- Goal: first $5k through a micro offer, i.e. 5 people paying about $1,000 for a service, plus an optional $10-20 micro product.
- Backstory (DEMO): tried seven models that stalled; lesson: every business needs traffic plus a place to take payment.
- Skip LLC/design/logo early; get customers first. Tool stack: writing/knowledge tool, store tool (Stan), newsletter tool (Beehiiv), forms, post scheduler, idea-research extension.
- 3-point marketing strategy to ask on every post/page/email: who can you help most, what can you help them achieve, why should they care. "You are the niche": serve people 1-2 steps behind you.
- Profile: photo clarity/contrast, bio of the form "I write about X, Y, Z so you can [outcome]" (learn in public), avoid "I help X do Y" templates; link to newsletter; pinned post = best-performing, origin story, or beginner action post; clean timeline.
- Offer creation recipe: copy what already sells, study and buy 3-5 competing products, build your best-of version, test on yourself, work close with first customers, improve and raise price.
- Low-friction funnel: qualification form (not a calendar link) in bio, newsletters, post ends; questions on goal, current state, target state, struggle, seriousness 1-10, readiness. Form acts as a filter and warms the lead.
- Outreach script: start from their interest; share one expert tip; soft mention of your service; follow up with a useful link after 2-3 days, once more a week later; then leave it.
- Micro product example: a $10 resume download that earned about $1k; or ebook/video/template. Micro product can funnel to the micro service. Landing page: pain, transformation, how it works, benefits, summary.
- Lead magnet: swipe file, template, checklist, ebook, or a repurposed best newsletter; beginner-level and action-oriented; free download builds authority and lets later posts go deeper.
- Growth mechanics [A]: replies (reply to posts that spark discussion; talk about your own experience, not "great post"; mix small, medium, large accounts), quote posts, reposts, manual DMs; short form to long form (grow on the easier platform then funnel to harder ones); curation/remixing/crediting bigger accounts; threads compiling others' top posts with tags; mastermind-style tribes of 5-10 peers who engage daily (Twitter list + group chat).
- Paid growth (OPINION, controversial): pay mid-sized accounts for reposts/comments of genuinely good content; content must be strong; plug a lead magnet underneath; avoid shoutouts and follower ads. Budget he cites: $250-2,000 per 30 reposts from accounts of 20k-80k.
- Emulation: copy structure or content, not both from one source. First 10 posts, 3 threads, first newsletter via "Pain & Process" (pain intro + steps + link to offer).

## 4. Full guide: how to start writing long form (2026-01-13) [N][I]
- Long-form is rising; he's bullish on Substack, X articles, YouTube, podcasts. Writing = thinking = questioning (what/how/why).
- Process: choose idea, ask 3+ questions, draft without judging, reread as a scrolling reader, object and expand, format for engagement.
- Piece anatomy: 1 topic idea (title), 3+ key point ideas (section heads), 3-5 filler ideas per key point; story shape problem -> insight -> solution. Quality is downstream of ideas; writer's block = missing ideas, so go consume/walk/talk to AI.
- Topic sources: content map, best performing posts, top posts from favorite accounts, idea-dense old books/curated blogs. Balance reach, relevance, resonance, and performance vs excitement. Keeps 5-10 candidate topics.
- Weekly outline early in the week; constraints help creativity; collect ideas from unrelated reading and synthesize (DEMO: example piece built from two articles).
- Idea types: starters (big ideas, quotes, stats, pains, personal experience) and explainers (anecdotes, concepts, insights, metaphors, actions, examples, what/how/why).
- Use AI to dissect great writing (macro and micro structure) rather than to write for you; prompt provided to analyze a piece's structure.
- Hook: show what most people get wrong, via personal story or direct statement; add a big-idea micro summary, example, stat or quote; 200-500 words.
- Three newsletter frameworks: Pain & Process (beginner); Pain, Concept, Process (intermediate; a deeper concept adds novelty); Perspective, Advantage, Gamify (advanced; common view vs. better view vs. step-by-step game, using flow psychology).

## 5. Turn 1 idea into 1000 (2025-09-13) [I][A]
- Brand = a curated collection of your best ideas in your words; best speakers repeat 5-10 core arguments. Ideas should sit where performance and excitement overlap.
- Keep an "idea museum" (swipe file) and write ideas down immediately. Find 3-5 idea-dense sources: old/little-known books, curated blogs/accounts, 5 heavy-hitting social accounts.
- Same idea, many structures: collect 15+ post structures; rewrite one idea in several (observation hook vs. list). Exercise: dissect 3 posts (AI prompt on idea, sentence structure, word choice, why they work), then rewrite 3 ideas using those structures.

## 6. The Content Map (2025-08-02) [I][A]
- Posts are puzzle pieces of a map, not darts. People follow for entertainment/education, topic interest, or belief you can change their life; the third matters most.
- Mission via anti-vision (life you reject, lowest point, worst self) and vision (what you do, skills, mindset, ideal life). Brand needs an enemy to rally against. Vision/anti-vision = your niche; attract people 1-2 steps behind.
- Map: dots for anti-vision and vision; 2-3 broad territories; 3-5 sub-topics each; 3-5 specific ideas per sub-topic (pain points, how-tos, lessons, why care, harsh truths, popular ideas, contrarian opinions); then draw connections across topics to see infinite combinations. Do on paper; treat as a magnet for ideas, not an exact list.
