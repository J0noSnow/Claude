## 1. "you weren't born to be an NPC" (2025-06-02, Substack)
Gist: Why most people run on scripts, and a 3-part recipe (awareness, access, agency) for becoming the "main character".
- [I] Hook = confessional childhood memory ("judgmental kid"), then a vivid, repetitive daily-routine list that dramatizes the problem. Opens with an epigraph quote. DEMO of style.
- [I] Naming pattern: borrowed pop-culture label (NPC / main character energy) + a borrowed academic model (first-tier vs second-tier consciousness / Spiral Dynamics) = fresh framing of an old idea. OPINION/technique.
- [I] Triads and 2x2s: awareness/access/agency; intelligence x agency matrix (four boxed outcomes); goals sorted easy/difficult/impossible; "good science" in 4 parts (goal, experiment, experience, confirmation). He turns every argument into a numbered, nameable structure.
- [I] Metaphor stack: success as a blank map with an invisible pin; learned-helplessness dog study as evidence; lighthouse-in-storm for iteration. Analogy plus one science story per section.
- [I] Argument arc: problem, why (3 causes), a "revolt", framework, call to experiment. Ends with "you're on your own" rather than a how-to.
- [A/N] Mid-letter inline plug for free letters vs paid members (soft CTA); P.S. "Note for this week" promotes a 60-day personal brand challenge (one lesson + one 30-60 min action per day). [L] DEMO: paid-challenge pitch sits in a footer note, not the body.
- [L/C] Challenge positioning: daily micro-lesson + action, ends with "monetize, traffic, trust".
- Exercise: write your own "default path" list in 10 clipped lines, then name the 3 ingredients needed to leave it.
- Form: ~2,000 words, short paragraphs, headers as labels, images of diagrams, no emoji.

## 2. "AI will never replace true creators" (2025-08-14, Substack)

Be consistent enough that a new reader can binge your archive in a week; "post 10 times and quit" does not work. OPINION.

- [I] Idea generation: he reverse-engineered his own idea-finding process into an AI "thought partner" prompt that asks him questions. DEMO.
- [C] 80/20 of prompting: documents of your process = detailed operating manuals, not 1-3 sentence prompts.
- Exercise: write one-sentence enemy + one-sentence shared vision, then check your last 5 posts against them.
- Form: blunt hook ("Most creators will fail..."), "stick with me" asides, bold numbered tactics, ends with "letter is getting long, rest later" (cliffhanger/serial). Mid-letter "if you like this, like this" engagement nudge.

## 5. How Smart Creators Will Make Money In 2026 (You're Early) (undated, ~May 2025)
Gist: Prediction that courses become "education + AI execution" products; step-by-step to build one from your own process.

He shares a "prompt that makes prompts".
- [C] Use someone else's process: feed an expert video/PDF to AI, extract instructions; also buy a successful competitor's product, have AI analyze its structure, and use that to outline your own. DEMO/OPINION.

- [L] Placement: footer lists upcoming paid challenge date with early-bird discount, plus latest paid posts (mega guide to digital products, sales page how-to, launch strategy next). Paid Substack posts used as teaching content for the paid-offer topic. DEMO.
- [I] Argument structure: three beliefs as numbered premises, then a vivid concrete example (YouTube course) before the how-to.
- Exercise: write step-by-step instructions for one task you do weekly as if for an intern.

## Cross-letter patterns for emulation
- Hook types used: contrarian claim; bleak routine list; confessional childhood memory; sarcastic cliche list; epigraph quote. Many reuse the same routine-list passage across letters and tweets (repurposing).
- Structure: short problem opener, named framework (triads, levels, cycles), metaphor per section, final imperative summary or serial teaser. Sign-offs: "– Dan", weekend wishes.
- Recurring vocabulary (brand glossary): NPC / main character, default path, eternal markets, level of mind, vision/anti-vision, purpose-path-priority, specialized generalist, you are the niche, create or be created.
- CTAs: mostly end-of-letter P.S. plus one soft mid-letter plug; paid ladder is cheap course, community, masterclass, software; deadline or price-doubling cited.
- Letters are free on the philosophical topic; tactical depth (mega guides, sales pages) sits in paid Substack posts.
