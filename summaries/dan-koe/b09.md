## 1. "Purpose & Profit: A Guide To Discovering Your Life's Work" (2025-05-25) - free short book serialised on Substack
Gist: A philosophical book-length argument that the future belongs to self-employed "creators" who merge purpose and profit; writing + audience + a self-made product is the practical route.
Form notes: ~25k words, free, with a table of contents of 12 chapters each with a one-line promise; intro disclaims "not a practical business book", asks to read twice (consume, then digest); pitches paperback + audio + subscription up top; ends with a values call ("Do it all. Write. Design. Market. Sell."). Heavy aphorism cadence, long chapters broken by bold keyword paragraphs.
**[I] Idea-generation & philosophy craft**
- Builds everything from a few recurring concepts and reuses them as names: job vs career vs calling (3 tiers, each defined with "a calling is..." anaphora); four levels of purpose (survival, status, creativity, contribution); "problems are infinite, problems are soluble"; entropy/boredom/anxiety vs "the edge of your abilities"; vision + anti-vision; "Nature's Compass" (his coined name for act-sense-compare-steer loop). OPINION/DEMO: he coins a label for every loop.
- Argument pattern: take a loaded word people dislike (money, entrepreneurship, sales, persuasion), redefine it as a mindset/neutral tool, then show the cost of rejecting it. Includes "gives permission" move ("I am giving you permission to pursue status").
- Borrows from many thinkers (Deutsch, Wiener, Wilber/holons, Csikszentmihalyi, Watts) and re-skins the ideas through his own goal; he explicitly says ideas change when refracted through a viewpoint.
- Plan-building scaffold he lists: anti-vision, vision, mission, standards, goals (decade to day, "stubborn on vision, loose on details"), projects, constraints, levers, challenge, curiosity. Exercise: keep a running note of experiences you never want to repeat; spend 30 min writing a minimum viable vision.
- Lost > interested > obsessed > repeat cycle (a 3-phase life cycle). Exercise: when lost, experiment inward, outward, downward (go deep), upward (monetise).
**[A][N] Writing**
- Writing is the "meta skill": thinking, learning, earning in one. Write in public, get feedback, error-correct. OPINION.
- DEMO process: outline first (thoughts, topics, sections), let it sit, add ideas as you read/watch ("DJs with ideas"), refine to nearly done, then publish regardless of feeling. "If you can, write every morning with your ideal future in mind."
- Practice recipe: pick topic; outline problem, goal, process between, pain points, benefits; structure as line of reasoning for one specific person.
**[L][C] Offer framework (five questions)**: who can you help most (past self), what problem (and awareness level: unaware > problem > solution > product > most aware; guess the majority level), where do they want to be (specific transformation: "ten fewer negative thoughts by tomorrow" beats "revolutionise your mind"), when will they get results (a timeframe forces clarity), why should they care (list pains and benefits, amplify pain). Then "your process is the product" - create it by reflecting on how you solved it or by experiment; example of buying many planners, mixing best parts. OPINION with method.
**[L][C] Self-monetisation ladder**: "You are the niche" - don't choose a niche, become it; it evolves as you do. Media company first (write free posts, newsletters), then small digital books/guides, then answer questions, then paid community with weekly articles, templates, live calls. DEMO (own story): ~5 years of failures (photography, web design, minimalist wallets), job in web design, side websites for family friends > lead-gen flows, left job in ~8 months; then free posts/articles/newsletters > small digital books > paid community > first book (The Art of Focus) > writing software company. Cites Justin Welsh: write publicly, consulting first, then self-paced course; $8M revenue/92% margin one-person business (claimed).
- "Offer" = media is paid in attention, product in money; package both as value exchange.
- Persuasion is the most ethical power; media is how you reach people at scale; own your audience instead of renting someone's.
**Practice prompts**: Write the five-question offer for one idea. Redefine one word your readers distrust. Name your own cycle/stage model in three tiers.

## 2. "You don't need a niche, you need a point of view" (2025-07-31)
Gist: Creators who are content factories get replaced; your lens (values, goals, experience) is the unreplicable niche, and novelty comes from refracting ideas through it.
Form: ~1.5k words, short punchy one-line paragraphs, hook = bold diagnosis ("glorified search engines"), H3 sections with a Naval quote at top, ends with 5-step "something to try" list and a soft close. Mid-letter self-links to his other guides (internal linking). Short sign-off "- Dan".
- [I] Chain: experiences > identity > goals/values > point of view > what you filter > decisions > reinforces identity. "Ideas transform when they pass through different points of view" (his refraction metaphor; example: compound interest as wealth vs habit, James Clear). Pattern: a quoted authority + his own coinage + concrete example. OPINION.
- [I][A] Specialist knowledge repositories are commoditised; people "follow humans not dictionaries"; want raw opinion, clarity over information, story/drama. OPINION.
- [A] Attention runs on novelty; novelty is produced by pattern recognition driven by a goal (reading a book with the aim of making money vs being happier yields different ideas). "Excitement-first" content, not trend-chasing. Claims he could write a persuasive post on topics outside his lane (crypto) because interests are persuaded, not innate.
- [A][N] Brand = what you are associated with over time; unify content under your ideal lifestyle (vision) as north star. Adlerian "lifestyle" idea.
- Concrete routine (OPINION, but is his method): (1) don't pick a topic, list everything important to your ideal life; (2) daily curation of ideas from books/videos/podcasts/articles, note what excites you; (3) post them as short-form like a note-taking feed; (4) each week pick the most interesting idea for a long-form newsletter or video; (5) fill it with personal experience, related ideas, tangible takeaways.
- If stuck: contemplate daily the life you do/don't want (walk and think only that) - clarity builds viewpoint.
- Exercise: write what you want/don't want daily on a walk for a week; then pick a mainstream idea and write it through your lens.
