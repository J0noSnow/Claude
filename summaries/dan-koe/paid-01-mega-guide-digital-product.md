## Product idea and research [C][L]
- First product should be beginner-level and simple: sell what people already buy, a 1% improvement, then add your own named mechanism. Advanced content can sit inside, but positioning and marketing target beginners because social audiences are mostly beginners. A beginner product can be promoted from any content topic. (OPINION + DEMO: his writing course is promoted from unrelated posts.)
- Pick a skill/interest from what you read, search, buy and do daily. Expect it to take days/weeks to firm up.
- Research order of reliability: (1) your own best-performing posts, threads and newsletters; (2) top-performing ideas from creators in your space (sort their videos/tweets by popularity, log title/thumbnail text/notes); (3) audience replies and recurring questions (question-posts work from roughly 2,500 followers); (4) 5-10 free calls with beginners found via thoughtful comments then a compliment DM; (5) buy/study mentors' courses, emails, funnels and sales pages as a builder, not a consumer.
- DEMO: his writing course came from a thread that did well; his one-person-business course came from a viral video, with a landing page built fast and selling started before the product was finished.
- Three delivery modes: tutorial (skill), program (step-by-step regimen), system (your own method). Competition is a good sign; people buy multiple courses on one topic.

## Niche and avatar [A][I]
- Don't start with a niche; start with who you can help. All goals fall in health, wealth, relationships, happiness; your angle is your route to one of them.
- Make yourself the avatar: starting point, what triggered change, ideal day, goals achieved that others want, how the product topic bridges start to now, and why the audience should care (benefits, not features).
- You get expertise by helping real customers, not only yourself.

## Marketing strategy components [L]
1. Angle tied to a core desire (career, skill, acceptance, companionship, enjoyment, status, freedom from fear).
2. Clear, believable goal: 1-2 steps ahead, with a timeframe and a number (examples from his products: 10x creative output in 7 days; build an audience in 30 days).
3. Transformation framing over features.
4. Bonuses (templates, checklists, community, coaching call, training video); also use as launch incentives or for testimonials.
5. Pricing: impulse-buy range (under about $225, often $100-150; beginners start lower). Price rises act as scarcity events alongside the launch.
6. Risk reversal: payment plans, guarantee, social proof, tied to the clear goal (cohort version: roll over free if goal missed).

## Mechanism [C][I]
- Mechanism = your unique system/process; systems sell. Build it by listing steps, running them yourself 1-2 weeks, refining, turning into an AI prompt/template, then giving it a compelling name (draft ~5 names, sleep on it).
- Marketing comes before product outline so the product is built to deliver the promise. Don't overpromise.

## Delivery [C][L]
- Outline: where they are, where they want to be, what bridges the gap (skills, beliefs, habits) -> modules. Add progress tracking via projects/worksheets/templates.
- Sequence recommended: cohort first (higher price, scarcity, repeatable launches 3-5/year, can pre-sell and build weekly) -> spin off a cheaper standalone course (use testimonials, upsell to cohort) -> community last (needs bigger growing audience; he started his at ~50K followers growing ~5K/month). Spend roughly 6-12 months on each stage.
- Format: writing for theory + video for implementation; around $150 range.
- Write modules like newsletters; frameworks PAK (problem, amplify, key points) and AIDA.
- Tools: Stan for standalone products, Circle for community/cohort, Substack ok but takes 10% plus processing.
