## 1. "how to launch into a completely new life" (2025-05-03) - substack, free
Gist: Life runs in chapters (limbo, vision, flow, resistance); escape limbo by collecting vision, changing your information diet, then gamifying the pursuit.
- [I] OPINION: Frames a problem as a predictable 4-phase cycle, then names the phase the reader is stuck in ("most get stuck in limbo"). Reader feels diagnosed, then gets a way out.
- [I] OPINION: Counterintuitive step one ("let your life get worse"/contemplate the anti-vision) as a hook device; goals are born from an outcome you want to avoid.
- [I] Borrows authority (Aristotle's final cause, Adler's teleology, Peterson) to back a plain-language claim: "you are where you are because you want to be, unconsciously."
- [I] Metaphor stack: lost in a forest / map vs territory / video-game mini-map (dark = unexplored) / adding 2.5lb plates to the bar.
- [C/L] DEMO: Cross-promotes a free mini-course and an AI prompt inside the body (soft offers placed at relevant points, not a hard pitch).
- [N] Mid-letter one-line free-subscriber ask; ends with "let me know if it was helpful" (reply CTA).
- Framework: Gamify = hierarchy of goals + rules (what you won't sacrifice) + quantifiable daily feedback tasks (e.g. 1000 words/day); tutorial phase = start first, study later; stay at the edge of the unknown.
Exercise: Name which of the 4 phases you are in and write the "if I keep doing this, where do I end up in 5 years" paragraph.
Form: ~1,400 words; no-preamble opener on a claim; numbered sections with a bolded sub-step list; very short one-sentence paragraphs; ends with a casual sign-off "- Dan". Image on top, promo link as a blockquote right after the intro.

- [I] Pattern: pick a dense, obscure framework (AQAL), translate it into 4 plain boxes, then run one concrete hard problem through it with sample questions per box. This is "explain a big model through a worked example."
- [I] Hook: "you don't need to be smart to think like a genius" + contrasting smart/dumb people. Claim that clarity, not IQ, is the point.
- [I] OPINION: People preach the one quadrant they live in (capitalists, materialists, Stoics...). Naming the blind spots of each camp is a reusable argument pattern.
- [I] "Thinking is a conversation with yourself": idiotic = argues and ends; genius = covers perspectives and synthesizes. Coins crisp definitions.
- [I] Name-drops admired thinkers (Peterson, Watts, Schmachtenberger, Wilber) as credibility and lineage; epigraph quote opens a section.
- [L/C] DEMO: Footer links related reading and a paid-article/course bundle ("Launchpad"); mid-letter line "subscribe for free and paid articles".
Exercise: Take one fuzzy personal question and write 4 questions per quadrant; read your answers for contradictions.
Form: ~1,300 words; H2 sections with provocative titles ("Why Smart People Are Incredibly Dumb"); diagrams embedded; pull-quote epigraphs; "pull out pen and paper" interactive moment; closes with a challenge to go learn more, then related-reading links.

## 3. "I'm begging you to write more essays" (2026-04-02) - substack, free; promo for a 14-day content-system challenge
Gist: Social media and AI produce "fast content"; essays are slow content that develops thinking; write essays to think in public and to build a career in the coming "meaning economy."
- [A][I] OPINION: Article vs essay distinction (answers vs arguments; start with a conclusion vs figure it out; packages knowledge vs changes author's beliefs). Great positioning for an essay-first writer. AI cannot write one because it lacks a lived point of view.
- [A] OPINION: Rules for writing an essay: write to discover not perform; one main idea; resist templates, debate with yourself first, structure later; ask "do I actually believe this?"; read essays; build a body of work not a content calendar.
- [A] OPINION: Publish where long-form is favored: X or Substack only.
- [I] Names and coins concepts: "fast content" vs "slow content", "epistemic commons" (water-supply metaphor), "meaning economy", "value creator". Moves from personal benefit to civilizational stakes (scale-up framing: self, then society).
- [I] Uses Schmachtenberger's three generator functions (rivalrous dynamics, substrate consumption, exponential tech) to explain why engagement content wins. Translates systems theory into creator terms.
- [I] Definition move: "meaning = experience of ordered consciousness"; challenge-skill level analogy.
- [N] DEMO: Admits he scrapped three drafts and the piece was hard to write; vulnerability that sells the thesis.
- [L/C] DEMO: Cohort/challenge reminder dropped in the middle of the letter after layer 1 (a break-line "Reminder:" block), and again at the end; deadline = start date (April 7), so urgency is natural.
Exercise: Write a 600-word essay on something that bothers you without knowing the conclusion; ask at the end what you now believe differently.
Form: ~2,200 words; roman-numeral sections (I-V); opens with a plea (3 short lines), states stakes, previews structure ("3 layers"); epigraph quotes under each heading; ends practically (bulleted rules, where to publish) then CTA.

## 4. "The influencer is dead. The Age of Experience is here." (2026-10-20) - substack, free; promotes his Eden tool and sprint course
Gist: People now relate to regular people with a story; become an "artisan" (social media subsidizes a body of work) via an idea museum, studying mid-sized creators, finding angles, then turning viral topics into products.
- [A] DEMO/OPINION: Hook is a surprising observation (non-technical mom clears a million-dollar launch with a 30-day challenge: waitlist, validated content, launch to waitlist + Instagram). Template for a first product.
- [I] Taxonomy naming: influencer (optimizes attention) vs personal brand (conversion) vs artisan (body of work). Defined by "what owns them." Great naming/framing example; closing line repeats the triad.
- [A][I] OPINION: "You are the niche"; the moat is an opinion; people follow perspectives, not information.
- [A] Step 1 Idea museum: capture every idea instantly; two boards, Validated Ideas (posts that went viral) and Favorite Ideas (ones you love); best post = merge of both through your point of view.
- [A][I] Step 2 Idea-dense sources: old/obscure books, curated blogs/accounts, a short list of creators you check instead of the feed. DEMO: he keeps creators in a list and opens it when stuck.
- [A] Study 3 mid-sized creators (not celebrities) and reverse-engineer posts; take the principles, not the idea.
- [I][A] The 5-angle method for one idea: how would a thinker I love say it; to my past self; redefine it; what's the common view and where I disagree; why it matters to the everyday person. Example: "constraints don't make you creative, they make you decisive." DEMO evidence: his 2019 "get ahead of 99%" post became a 1.7M-view video; later Manson, Hormozi, Bilyeu used the same title; "nobody owns a topic."
- [L][C] Products = what people signal they want (viral topics) + a problem you solved. Example: low-ticket 30-day focus challenge community. Audience-first means demand is pre-tested.
- 30-day plan: W1 museum + 5-creator list + one idea/day; W2 study 3 creators, write 5 angles/day; W3 post and watch replies/questions; W4 turn the top question into a one-page free guide for emails, then a bigger paid version.
- Pasteable AI prompts (angle-finding, creator research) given in blockquotes; long prompt gated behind a link (lead magnet).
Exercises: start an idea museum with two boards; write the 5 angles for one saved idea.
Form: ~1,900 words; anecdote hook; epigraph quote; H3 headings; step lists; recap with weekly plan; tool plug (Eden) woven as "how I do it", disclosed as his product.

# Cross-letter patterns (for the playbook)
- Hooks: contrarian claim, plea ("I'm begging you"), surprising anecdote, named list of years, definition quote then flip, letter-to-past-self.
- Structure: epigraph quote, numbered steps with bolded step names, 1-sentence paragraphs, a named concept in every letter, a metaphor (tree, game, food, water), a recap or weekly plan, closing sign-off "- Dan".
- CTAs: soft inline product mentions where relevant, hard CTA at the end or after a mid-letter divider, usually with a deadline or start date; footers menu by intent; reply CTA in early letters; "What happened this week" block in older letters.
- Recurring ideas: vision/anti-vision, goals as lenses, entropy vs order of consciousness, flow (skill vs challenge), value creator, "you are the niche", build a body of work, start then learn, promote before you feel ready, one hour a day.
- Ladder: free social content then newsletter then micro product ($10-19) or service (4 calls at $750-1000) then course/community then bigger programs; validation via pre-sale; content topics from what already goes viral.
